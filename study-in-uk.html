Why study in the UK?

Over 600,000 international students pick the UK each year — for centuries-old universities, a master's you can finish in twelve months, and a degree that employers recognise anywhere in the world.

One-year master's degrees

Funding worth chasing

Eligibility Criteria for Studying in the UK

Every university sets its own bar, but this is the range UK institutions typically ask international applicants to meet.

  • Higher secondary completed with 60% – 80% or better
  • IELTS 6.0 – 6.5 overall, with no band under 5.5
  • Or TOEFL iBT 80+ / PTE 59+ / Duolingo (selected universities)
  • Most undergraduate courses are applied for through UCAS
  • A relevant bachelor's degree, usually 55% – 70% or a 2:1 equivalent
  • IELTS 6.5 overall with no band below 6.0
  • Personal statement, CV and letters of recommendation
  • GMAT or GRE for selected MBA and finance programmes

Application Requirements in the UK

Completed application form

Through UCAS for undergraduate courses, and direct to the university for most master's.

Academic transcripts

Marksheets and certificates from every institution you have attended.

English test scores

IELTS, TOEFL, PTE or Duolingo — or a waiver if you were taught in English.

Personal statement

Your reasons for the course, the university and the UK, written in your own voice.

CV & recommendations

A résumé, plus academic or professional references for postgraduate courses.

Proof of funds

Tuition for year one plus nine months of living costs, held for 28 straight days.

Student Visa for the UK (Student Route)

You can apply once your university issues your CAS, up to six months before the course starts. Decisions from outside the UK usually arrive within three weeks of your biometrics appointment.

GBP 558Student visa application fee
GBP 776 / yearImmigration Health Surcharge (IHS)
GBP 13,761 / 10,539Maintenance funds — London / outside London, 9 months

Documents required for your visa application

  • Valid passport
  • Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS)
  • Proof of funds held for 28 consecutive days
  • Immigration Health Surcharge payment receipt
  • English proficiency score, IELTS UKVI where required
  • Academic transcripts & certificates
  • Tuberculosis (TB) test certificate
  • ATAS certificate, for selected research subjects

Top Student-Friendly Cities in the UK

London is the obvious draw, but the cities beyond it cost far less and still put you inside a Russell Group campus.

London, England

Dozens of universities inside one city, the deepest graduate job market in Europe, and part-time work that is genuinely easy to find.

The biggest student population outside London, with rent at roughly half the London rate and a booming tech and media sector.

FAQs

Can I study in the UK without an English proficiency test?

Sometimes. A number of universities waive the test if your previous qualification was taught and assessed in English — but the waiver is decided case by case, so plan for IELTS unless it is confirmed in writing.

What is the Russell Group?

An association of 24 research-intensive UK universities, widely used as shorthand for the country's leading research institutions.

How long can I stay after I graduate?

The Graduate Route gives you 18 months of unsponsored work for applications made from January 2027, and three years if you completed a doctorate.

How much money do I need to show for the visa?

Your first year's tuition, minus any deposit paid, plus GBP 1,529 a month for London or GBP 1,171 a month elsewhere, for up to nine months. The money must sit in the account for 28 consecutive days.

How many hours can I work while studying?

Up to 20 hours a week during term for a degree-level course, and full time during official holidays and after your course finishes.

How long does the Student visa take?

Applications from outside the UK are normally decided within three weeks of your biometrics appointment, and priority services can shorten that.
