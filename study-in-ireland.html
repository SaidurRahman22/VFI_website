Student Visa in Ireland

Courses longer than 90 days need a Long-Stay "D" study visa. Apply about three months before you plan to travel — processing usually takes 4 to 8 weeks.

€60Single-entry visa fee
€100Multi-entry visa fee
€10,000Proof of funds, per year of study

Documents required for your visa application

  • Completed visa application form
  • Valid passport
  • Letter of admission from your university
  • Evidence of sufficient funds
  • Proof of fees paid for the year
  • Medical insurance (minimum €25,000)
  • Police clearance certificate
  • English proficiency score card

FAQs

How long does the student visa last for a master's in Ireland?

Your permission covers the length of your course. You then register for an IRP card in Ireland, renewed each year, and can apply for the Third Level Graduate Programme afterwards.

What does an Irish student visa cost?

€60 for a single-entry visa and €100 for multi-entry. You'll also pay an IRP registration fee after you arrive.

How much proof of funds do I need?

€10,000 for each year of study, on top of the tuition fees paid for that year.

How long does visa processing take?

Usually 4 to 8 weeks. Apply around three months before your intended travel date to be safe.

Can I work part-time while studying?

Yes — 20 hours per week during term, and 40 hours per week during official holiday periods.

When can I apply for the post-study work visa?

After your results are published. Master's graduates get up to 24 months; bachelor's graduates get 12 months.

Which Irish cities are most and least affordable?

Dublin is the most expensive. Galway, Limerick and Cork are noticeably more affordable, mostly because of rent.

When should I start looking for accommodation?

As soon as you accept your offer. Dublin housing is tight — apply for university accommodation the day it opens.
