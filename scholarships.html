Know your options

Types of scholarships

Most students qualify for more than one category. Knowing which is which is half the battle — the other half is applying properly.

Merit-based

Merit & academic excellence awards

Decided on your grades, test scores and academic achievements. Competitive, but the most widely available category — and often the fastest to secure.

25%–100% tuition waiver
Need-based

Need-based financial aid

Assessed on family income and demonstrated financial need rather than marks alone. Requires income proof and, in some cases, a supporting statement.

Partial to full cost of attendance
Government-funded

Country & government-funded schemes

National programmes such as Chevening, Fulbright and Australia Awards. Long lead times and strict windows, but the most generous funding on offer.

Full tuition, stipend, flights & insurance
Entrance award

University entrance awards

Applied automatically or with a short form when you accept an offer. No separate essay in most cases — you simply need to apply early enough to be considered.

Typically 10%–50% off tuition
Where you're headed

Scholarships by destination

A snapshot of the schemes we apply to most often. Eligibility, value and deadlines change each cycle — we confirm the current rules before you invest time in an application.

  • University merit scholarships — dean's and international excellence awards granted with your admission offer at both public and private universities, usually as a percentage cut off tuition.
  • Graduate assistantships (TA / RA) — teaching or research posts at master's and doctoral level that typically combine a tuition waiver with a monthly stipend in exchange for departmental work.
  • Fulbright Foreign Student Program — the US government's flagship exchange award for master's and doctoral study, administered in India by USIEF, covering tuition, living costs, airfare and insurance.
  • Tata Scholarship at Cornell University — a need-based endowed fund for Indian undergraduates admitted to Cornell, covering the gap between family contribution and the full cost of attendance.
  • Chevening Scholarships — the UK government's fully funded award for one-year taught master's degrees, judged on leadership, networking and a clear career plan alongside academics.
  • Commonwealth Scholarships — master's and PhD funding for students from eligible Commonwealth countries, with tuition, a living allowance and return airfare covered.
  • GREAT Scholarships — run by the British Council with participating UK universities, offering a fixed contribution towards tuition for selected countries and subject areas.
  • University excellence awards — institution-funded schemes such as Bristol's Think Big and Glasgow's World Changers, applied for separately once you hold an offer.
  • Vanier Canada Graduate Scholarships — doctoral funding of CAD 50,000 a year for three years, open to international candidates and nominated by the university, not applied for directly.
  • Canada Graduate Scholarships – Master's (CGS-M) — a federal tri-agency award worth CAD 27,000 for twelve months; eligibility is restricted, so we confirm your status before you build an application around it.
  • Ontario Graduate Scholarship — a provincial merit award for master's and doctoral students at participating Ontario universities, with a share of places reserved for international students.
  • Lester B. Pearson International Scholarship — the University of Toronto's undergraduate award covering tuition, books, incidental fees and residence for four years, requiring a school nomination.
  • Australia Awards Scholarships — long-term development awards from the Australian government for students from participating countries, covering tuition, airfare, an establishment allowance and living costs.
  • Destination Australia Program — annual funding for students enrolling at a regional campus, designed to spread demand beyond Sydney and Melbourne.
  • Research Training Program (RTP) — government support for higher degree by research candidates, offsetting tuition and, for many recipients, adding a living stipend.
  • University merit awards — vice-chancellor's and international excellence scholarships at institutions such as Melbourne, Sydney and Monash, usually a percentage discount on tuition.
  • Government of Ireland International Education Scholarship — a one-year award pairing a EUR 10,000 stipend with a full fee waiver from the host institution, open to students from outside the EU/EEA.
  • Government of Ireland Postgraduate Scholarship Programme — competitive research funding for master's and PhD candidates, now administered by Research Ireland.
  • University global excellence scholarships — merit awards from UCD, Trinity College Dublin and University of Galway, applied for after you receive an offer.
  • Faculty and course-level awards — smaller subject-specific bursaries in business, computing and engineering that attract far fewer applicants than the headline schemes.

Scholarship FAQs

The questions students ask us first, answered straight.

When should I start applying?

Twelve months before your intake for the big government schemes, which close very early, and alongside your university applications for entrance awards. Starting late is the single most common reason a strong profile goes unfunded.

Do I need a scholarship to get a student visa?

No. Visa officers want proof that your tuition and living costs are covered — a scholarship simply reduces the amount you need to show. Loans, savings and sponsor income all count.

Can I hold more than one scholarship?

Often yes, but not always. Many university awards stack with external funding, while most fully funded government schemes forbid it outright. We read the terms of each award before you accept anything.

Are scholarships only for academic toppers?

No. Need-based aid, regional and sports awards, subject bursaries and assistantships all weigh things other than marks — work experience, leadership, financial circumstances and research fit. We have funded plenty of mid-band profiles.

Do you charge a fee for scholarship help?

Scholarship guidance is part of the VFI application service — your profile assessment and shortlist cost nothing. We will always tell you upfront if a funder itself charges an application fee.
