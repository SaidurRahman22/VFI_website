Work rights while studying

How many hours you may work is set by your visa, not your employer or your university. These are the current headline limits for our four biggest destinations — always confirm the rule that applies to your own visa before you accept a role, as governments revise these caps.

  • USA (F-1): up to 20 hours a week of on-campus work while classes are in session, and full-time during official breaks. Off-campus work needs authorisation — CPT during your course, or OPT for up to 12 months after it, with a 24-month extension for eligible STEM degrees.
  • UK (Student route): up to 20 hours a week in term time on a degree-level course, and full-time during vacations and once your course has finished. Sub-degree courses are capped lower, at 10 hours a week.
  • Canada (study permit): up to 24 hours a week off campus during academic sessions, and full-time during scheduled breaks such as the summer and winter holidays.
  • Australia (Student visa subclass 500): up to 48 hours per fortnight while your course is in session, and unrestricted hours during scheduled course breaks.
Good to know

Internship FAQs

The questions students ask us most before committing to a placement route.

Are internships paid?

Some are, some are not. Co-op work terms and corporate placements are usually paid at local rates; research, NGO and short summer placements are often unpaid or carry a small stipend. We tell you which is which before you apply.

Do they count towards a degree?

Only if the placement is part of your programme. Embedded placements and co-op terms carry academic credit and appear on your transcript; an internship you arrange independently in the holidays normally does not.

When should I apply?

Decide on a placement-based course at the same time as you shortlist universities. For roles you apply to yourself, start six to nine months ahead — large employers close summer applications well before the summer.

Do you place students directly?

No. VFI is not an employment agency and we never promise a job. We steer you towards universities with strong placement records and career teams, and we prepare your application so you compete well for the roles they open.

Do internships help with PR or a work visa?

Indirectly. Local experience and an employer who knows you make a post-study work visa or sponsorship far more achievable, but no internship grants residency on its own. Each country sets its own immigration criteria.
