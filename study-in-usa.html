Eligibility Criteria for Studying in the USA

Requirements differ by level and university, but this is the baseline most institutions expect from applicants.

  • Completed 12 years of schooling with a good overall average
  • English proficiency: IELTS 6.0+ / TOEFL 79+ / PTE 53+
  • SAT or ACT for selective universities
  • Proof of funds for tuition and living costs
  • A recognised bachelor's degree (many universities accept 3-year degrees)
  • English proficiency: IELTS 6.5+ / TOEFL 90+ / PTE 58+
  • GRE or GMAT where the program requires it
  • Work experience for MBA and some management programs

FAQs about studying in the USA

Is IELTS mandatory to study in the USA?

Not always. Most universities accept IELTS, TOEFL, PTE or Duolingo, and some waive the requirement if your previous education was in English.

Can I work while studying?

Yes — up to 20 hours per week on campus during term, and full time in holidays. CPT and OPT allow paid work in your field.

Is a 3-year bachelor's degree accepted?

Many US universities now accept 3-year degrees for master's admission. We'll tell you which of your target schools do.

How long does the F-1 visa take?

It depends on appointment availability. Apply as soon as you receive your I-20 — you can do so up to 365 days before your course starts.

Do I need GRE or GMAT?

Many programs are now test-optional, but a good score still strengthens your application and your scholarship chances.

How much money do I need to show?

Enough to cover one year of tuition plus living costs, as stated on your I-20. We help you structure this correctly.
